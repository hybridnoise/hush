Guide

The sound doesn't punish barking. It interrupts it, so you get a moment of quiet to reward. Rewards are what change the habit.

Charge the Sound

Days 1–3

Before you use the sound on barking, teach the dogs that it means good things.

  1. With calm dogs, tap Interrupt, then give a treat right away.
  2. Do 10 of these, 2–3 times a day.
  3. It's working when they look at you or run over at the sound.

When They Bark

  1. Tap Interrupt once. Repeating it turns it into background noise.
  2. Wait for quiet. Good Quiet fills up as you reach your goal.
  3. Tap Good Quiet (it clicks) and treat within a second or two.
  4. Start with a 1–3 second goal. Make it longer as they improve.

Add the Word

Once they go quiet reliably, say "Quiet" just before you tap Good Quiet. Over time the word alone will work.

Bark Detection

  • Turn on Listen for Barks in Train. Hush plays Interrupt after 1–3 barks (you choose). Then it waits 15 seconds before interrupting again.
  • It listens for quiet too. When the dogs have been quiet long enough, Click Now lights up. Tap it and give a treat.
  • You still need to be there. The reward is what teaches them. Without one, the interrupt is just noise they learn to ignore.
  • Keep Hush open with the screen on. iPhone turns off the mic in the background or when the phone locks.
  • Voices, the TV or a door slam can set it off. If that happens, lower Sensitivity.

Make It Easier

  • Find the trigger: the window, the doorbell, passing dogs or boredom.
  • Block window views, and use white noise to cover outside sounds.
  • More exercise and chew toys cut down barking from boredom.
  • Don't yell. To a dog, it sounds like you're barking along.

Be Kind to Their Ears

  • Never hold the phone near a dog's head.
  • Don't use the sound as punishment, and don't play it nonstop.
  • If a dog seems scared (cowering, hiding), stop and switch to a lower, softer tone.
  • Sound won't fix barking from anxiety, such as when you leave. Talk to your vet or a behaviorist about that.
