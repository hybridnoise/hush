Volume

Most adults can't hear above about 15–17 kHz, but kids often can. iPhone speakers get weak above about 18 kHz, and no phone can make a true "silent" whistle (those go above 23 kHz). Pick a tone your dogs react to. It doesn't need to be silent.

No sound? Turn up the volume and check that the ring/silent switch isn't on silent.

How it works

The sound doesn't punish barking. It interrupts it, and that gives you a moment of quiet to reward. Rewarding quiet is what changes the habit. Reward-based training is linked to less stress and faster learning than harsh methods.

1 · Charge the sound (first 3 days)

Before you use it on barking, teach the dogs that the sound means good things.

  1. With calm dogs, tap Interrupt, then give a treat right away.
  2. Do 10 of these, 2–3 times a day.
  3. It's working when the dogs look at you or run to you at the sound.

2 · When they bark

  1. Tap Interrupt once. Repeating it over and over turns it into background noise.
  2. Wait for quiet. The Good button fills up as you reach your quiet goal.
  3. Tap Good quiet (it clicks) and give a treat within a second or two.
  4. Start with a 1–3 second goal. Make it longer as they improve.

3 · Add the word

Once they go quiet reliably, say "Quiet" just before you tap Good. Over time the word alone will work.

Bark detection

  • Turn it on in the Train tab. Hush listens with the mic and plays Interrupt when it hears barking (after 1–3 barks, your choice). Then it waits 15 s before it will interrupt again.
  • It also listens for quiet. When the dogs have been quiet for your goal, Click now lights up. Tap it and give a treat.
  • You still need to be there. The reward is what teaches them. Interrupts with nobody around to reward turn into noise the dogs learn to ignore.
  • Keep Hush open with the screen on. iPhone turns off the mic when the app is in the background or the phone locks.
  • Voices, the TV or a door slam can set it off. If it does, lower Sensitivity.

Make it easier

  • Find the trigger: window, doorbell, passing dogs, boredom.
  • Block window views and use white noise for outside sounds.
  • More exercise and chew toys reduce barking from boredom.
  • Don't yell. To a dog, it sounds like you're barking along.

Be kind to their ears

  • Never hold the phone near a dog's head.
  • Don't use the sound as punishment, and don't play it constantly.
  • If a dog seems scared (cowering, hiding), stop and use a lower, softer tone.
  • Sounds don't help barking from anxiety, such as when you leave. Talk to your vet or a behaviorist about that.
